A sweet shop that begins on a dairy farm

Dudhaloy means the house of milk. We are the sweet shop of R.M. Dairy Farm & Agro Fisheries in Moulvibazar, so every sweet on our counter starts with milk from our own cows.

Our story

Draft copy, to confirm with the owners

For years our farm in Adampur has raised dairy cows the slow way: clean feed, open space, and caretakers who know each animal. The milk was good enough that people kept asking where they could taste it.

Dudhaloy is our answer. Instead of selling the milk on, we turn it into the sweets Bengal has always made with it: rasmalai, roshogolla, sandesh, chomchom and mishti doi. The milk travels a few kilometres, not a few hundred, and goes into the pan the same morning.

We make small batches every day, so what you buy today was made today.

Dudhaloy sweets / Our farm

R.M. Dairy Farm & Agro Fisheries

The farm behind every Dudhaloy sweet. An integrated agro complex in Adampur, Moulvibazar, where the cows, the fields and the ponds look after each other.

Our cattle are raised with dignity

Any life placed in our care is a trust, and we honour that trust in the way we feed, shelter and protect them.

Each animal is looked after with patience and respect. They get clean feed, fresh water and the attention of skilled caretakers who know them well, not just by name but by nature.

We don't rush or force growth. We nurture it, gently and consistently. In return our cattle stay healthy, calm and productive, and give milk of excellent quality, with barakah in both taste and yield.

Sustainable sister project

Cornfields, and nothing goes to waste

We grow corn around the farm. The cattle eat the residue with the grass we grow, which improves milk and meat. Their manure is composted and goes back to fertilise the cornfields.

Most of the remaining animal and plant waste becomes biogas.

Sustainable sister projects

Around the cattle sheds, each project feeds the next.

Sustainable sister project

Our fishery ponds

A symbiotic piscine culture

Three fishery ponds enrich the biodiversity of the farm. We rear species that prefer different depths, so they share each pond without competing.

The pond banks mimic the terrain many fruit trees need, so the ponds also make room for seasonal fruit, including our dragon fruit.

Sustainable sister project

Dragon fruit

An exotic plantation

Dragon fruit is still new to Bangladesh, and we are one of its proud pioneers who found success with it.

The plantation is modest, but more than enough for the local market. The fruit grows sweet and refreshing thanks to the thriving ecosystem it flowers in.

Sustainable sister project

Avian ecology

Pigeons, ducks and guineafowl

We started rearing birds alongside our livestock as an experiment. We never planned on poultry, but our pigeons and ducks have done better than we expected.

The birds bring life to the farm, add to its ecosystem, and keep pests off the fields.
